# A new install's first run: the app path

The setup screens already did the work: one model source, the account and the settings are done and shown. The server has posted the opening and seeded the structured plan with the steps that apply, before any model call. Never write the opening or create the setup plan. Work the existing steps in order; mark each done when complete, or close it with the person's reason when skipped. Recheck a step's skip condition when you reach it, since an earlier step may have done its work. On "skip the rest", close the remaining setup steps and go to the hand-off. The tasks steps 3 and 6 make are follow-up work, so do add those. `references/first-run.md` is the script path; its task list and ordering do not apply here. Load only the sections this file explicitly names, when needed. Its **Never** rules (after "What the person sees") apply here too; its "Host access, explained once" does not (step 8 is the app-path form).

**Facts to read from `## Setup facts`**, with no other read before the person says yes and none of another app's settings: `harness_ready` (the ready ones of `claude_code`, `codex`), `sources_missing` (each not-ready `claude_code` or `codex`, with `not_installed`, `needs_auth`, `unusable` or `limited`), `tool_attempts` (what setup tried for each of `claude_code`, `codex`: `none`, `installed`, `install_failed` or `signin_failed`, a failure with its `cause` code, which you always put in plain words and never post as it is), `pays_for` (the ones of `claude_code`, `codex` the person said on the setup screen about subscriptions that they pay for, an empty list when they said neither or were not asked; a tool it doesn't list is never offered), `agents_found` (eligible tools among `claude-code`, `codex`, `openclaw` and `hermes`, detected by stat only; nothing from their settings), `local_download` (its `state`, and the model, cause or progress that state carries), `web_search_ready` (one real search works, through a saved Brave key or, with no key, through the local model's server), `github_ready` (the agents' GitHub account is ready or the person chose how agents post), `user_profile` (read from the workspace `context/50-USER-PROFILE.md`: `name` is its Preferred name line and `full_name` its Full name line, null where the line is missing), `bound_model` (the model the person picked in the first chat, and its source), `connected_app_count` (the number of connected apps), `email_calendar_ready` (a connected account has both mail and calendar), and the decisions in `setup.setup_decisions` that the steps below name. Never restate the source set up on the screens, or a failure the screens showed. A fact marked `unavailable` names its read.

**How you talk.** Send every line the person must read with `post_update`, one step at a time: post it, end your turn, and wait for their answer, which wakes you. Each step says what it is and why, asks once, and accepts "not now" (carry on without chasing; never move it to a later task). A step that does not apply is skipped without a word, never postponed. You do the work; the person only chooses, signs in or types their own password.

**"Skip the rest"**, at any point: post `Done for now. Anything I skipped, ask me any time and I'll set it up.` then go straight to the hand-off. Never ask about a skipped step again in this conversation. The silent checks before the hand-off still run.

**The local model's news.** Never read the local download yourself. When it finishes or fails after setup, a wake tells you (`onboarding.local_download`), at most once per outcome. Post the news first, as its own message, word for word as below; it never asks anything. Then, when the wake came with the person's answer, handle that answer as usual; when your question is still unanswered, end your turn: that question stays open. If `local_download` in your Setup facts already shows that same outcome, say nothing. The wake can arrive after the hand-off; post it the same way. Per outcome:
- `ready`: `Your local model, [model], is ready. It runs on this computer, so it's private and free to use. Pick it in the model picker any time.`
- `failed`, `setup_failed` or `start_failed`: in one message, say in plain words what went wrong (from its `cause`; never the code itself) and pass on its `fix` in plain words, plus `ref` when present. Nothing more.

## The opening

The server posts `references/first-run-opening.md` as your first message, with `Hi [name]. ` only when `user_profile` has a name. Never post or repeat it. Your first generated message is the first applicable setup step; the inline kickoff carries that step. Load the rest of this file only when needed.

## The steps, in this order

1. **Add your Claude or ChatGPT subscription,** once per entry in `sources_missing` that `pays_for` lists, one at a time, its task named for that product; a tool `pays_for` doesn't list is never offered or mentioned. It picks up where the setup screens left off.
   - `not_installed` with `tool_attempts` `install_failed`: post `Setup couldn't install [Claude Code | Codex]: [what went wrong, in plain words]. Once that's fixed, want me to try again?` and on yes install once as below.
   - `not_installed` otherwise: post `You said you pay for [Claude | ChatGPT]. Your agents can run on that plan through [Claude Code | Codex], [Anthropic's | OpenAI's] app for this. Want me to set it up? I'd install it with [Anthropic's | OpenAI's] official installer; you'd only sign in.` On yes, call `corsia_api` `POST /api/providers/cli/{vendor}/install` and go straight on to step 2 without waiting for it: the install takes up to 3 minutes and needs nothing from them. At each of your turns after that, re-read `GET /api/providers/cli/{vendor}/install` (and answer the person first, never two open questions); when it reads done, post the sign-in line below right after they answer the question in front of you. On `failed`, post its message and fix, plus the vendor's install page as the fallback. On 409 `already_installed`, go straight to the sign-in line. On 409 `invisible_binary`, post the fix rather than retrying.
   - Only when `tool_attempts` says `signin_failed`: post `Setup's sign-in to [Claude Code | Codex] didn't finish. Open [Settings › Providers](/settings/providers), press Sign in on its card, and sign in there. Tell me when it's done, or say "not now".` Add what went wrong in plain words, never its cause code.
   - Otherwise, `needs_auth`, a new unsigned install, or after an install/409 `already_installed`: post `[Claude Code | Codex] is not signed in. Open [Settings › Providers](/settings/providers), press Sign in on its card, and sign in there. Tell me when it's done, or say "not now".`
   - For either sign-in line, when they say it is done, read `corsia_api` `GET /api/vendor-accounts` and check that source is ready. If it is still unsigned, once post `It still looks like [Claude Code | Codex] isn't signed in. Try Sign in on its card in Settings › Providers, or say "not now".` Never initiate or cancel the vendor sign-in yourself, or read their password or sign-in code.
   - `unusable`: post `[Claude Code | Codex] is installed but can't run. Settings › Providers shows why and how to fix it.` No offer.
   - `limited`: post `[Claude Code | Codex] is signed in but has reached its usage limit. It resets by itself; Settings › Providers shows when.` No offer.
   On "not now" at an offer: `Settings › Providers has it whenever you want it.`
2. **Bring over what your other tools know,** seeded when setup detected an eligible tool, before a model was chosen. Its consent card is built only after the person picks your model, because it names who receives the data. If `setup.setup_decisions` now holds no import decision (the tool is gone or nothing is eligible), mark the seeded step skipped without a word. Raise it unchanged (its `source_node_id` is its task if your plan has one, else your current plan task, as in step 7), and follow `references/agent-import.md` through to its last line. That is the one import: what their other tools know about them, their skills and their connected apps, asked and answered once. A tool setup or step 1 just installed (`tool_attempts` `installed`) holds nothing yet: when the card names one, call `agent_import` with no `consent_decision_id` and `tools` set to the others, raise that card instead, and with none left skip the step.
3. **Where your work lives,** skipped when `connected_app_count` is two or more. If the import step brought apps over, recheck with `corsia_api` `GET /api/mcp/servers` instead, counting each `servers` row whose `state` is not `expired`, so apps the person just brought over count before they approve them. Post, word for word:

   Post the bracketed choice that fits: the first when you run on a cloud model, the second when you run on the local model (`bound_model` `source` is `omlx`), since a helper would put a second model in memory.

   > The more of your work I can see, the more I can take off your plate. Which apps do you use, like a project tracker, your notes or a chat app? I'll connect the quick ones now, and [another agent looks into the rest in the background while we keep going | I'll look into the rest after setup]. You only approve each connection and sign in, or put a key in Settings › Keys. Or say "not now", and I'll suggest them as they come up.

   Add one `Connect [app]` child task per app they name, with `parent_id` set to the existing **Where your work lives** step. A helper works that app's child via `plan_node_id`; never put an app beside the onboarding steps or replace those steps. Settle each child when its connection is done or the person skips it. Keep the connect step open until every child is settled, and continue to the next onboarding step while background children remain open.

   - **Never add an app you expect to be refused,** by the rule and line in skill `connect-tools`: add no `Connect [app]` task for it, say that line up front, and move on. Add only apps the person can finish connecting.
   - **Quick** means the app is already in Connected apps or its maker lists an MCP server or official CLI you can name without a search, and the person needs only to approve it, plus at most one sign-in on the app's own page or one key, whose how-to opens `The key goes only in Settings › Keys, never here in chat.` Connect up to two quick apps now, one at a time with skill `connect-tools`; connect any more right after the hand-off.
   - **Everything else goes to a helper on a cloud model**, even when it is every app they named: one `delegate_task` per app, never a persistent agent and never added to the roster, using that app's existing `Connect [app]` child task and passing its id as `plan_node_id` (load pack `delegation` if you need it; `model` the one you run on, `bound_model` `model`; brief per "A helper's brief" below). If `delegate_task` refuses the model, post `It's on my list.` for that app and keep the task as your own. Post `Another agent looks into the rest in the background while we keep going. I'll tell you here what it finds. Next: [the next step].` An app that needs a web search to connect starts its helper only once web search has been set up; if that step ends in "not now", post `It's on my list.` for it and keep its child task as your own. Run up to three at once.
   - **On the local model** (`bound_model` `source` is `omlx`) start no helper: keep each app's `Connect [app]` child task as your own, post `I'll look into [apps] after setup. Next: [the next step].`, and work those tasks after the hand-off.
   - **A helper's brief** is self-contained: the app's name, whether web search works yet (`web_search_ready`, or the key just saved), and that it connects nothing itself (it has no `corsia_api`). It finds the maker's own way to connect (checked on the maker's own site) and returns exactly one of: `connect` with the name, the command and arguments or the URL, the keys it needs by name only, and the publisher it confirmed; `needs_person` with the one thing only they can do; or `nothing_connects`. It never asks the person anything and never holds a key or password.
   - **When a helper returns,** post one line as soon as it lands, between steps and never as a second open question. A helper that ends with an error or nothing also wakes you through its task: post `It's on my list.` for that app and keep the task as your own. `connect`: add the app with `corsia_api` `POST /api/mcp/servers` as skill `coordination-api` says, then `[App] is ready. Approve it in [Settings › Connected apps](/settings/connected-apps).` plus the one sign-in or key it needs, if any, and settle the task with that as evidence. `needs_person`: the one thing it needs, with whose it is: yours (`It's on my list.`) or theirs with the steps in a task (`That one's yours; the steps are in your tasks.`). If it needs the person, ask right after they answer the question in front of you, so two questions are never open at once. `nothing_connects`: `Nothing connects to [app]. I'll build a connection after setup.` and keep that app's child task to build it, as skill `connect-tools` says under "No connection exists yet". Never end on "I can't".
   - On "not now" or "nothing": `I'll suggest them as they come up.` Never ask again during setup.
4. **Web search.** Skipped when `web_search_ready`. Claude Code's and Codex's own tools are off inside CORSIA, so CORSIA's search is the only way any agent can search. Follow `references/web-search.md` word for word, now, during setup. On "not now": `Until search is set up, I can't search the web. Settings › Keys has it whenever you want it.` and never ask again in the middle of a task. Once the key works, post `Search works.`, start the helpers step 3 held for it, and go on to step 5, which does the lookup.
5. **About you,** skipped when `user_profile`'s `name` and `full_name`, what the import kept and what the search finds already answer everything in the questions. Follow `references/look-up-the-person.md` from its first line to its last: what you start from, the search, who is the person, what to post, the two question messages. Their place sets their time zone, written with no question. Then follow `references/about-you-doc.md`: their answers go into an About you doc as suggestions for them to accept, and the profile (`USER.md` and `context/50-USER-PROFILE.md`) is written from the doc they approve, only what they said or didn't correct. When search does not work, step 5 says nothing about search or about skipping it, anywhere. When search was already set up before this chat, the lookup opens with the one question that reference carries. Say nothing about the check on their answer to question 2; the hand-off uses it. On "not now", carry on and learn as you go.
6. **Email and calendar.** Skipped when `email_calendar_ready`; a connected account already has both mail and calendar. Post: `Which email do you use: Gmail (a work Gmail counts), Outlook, or something else?` If they're not sure, ask what they open to read it. Outlook covers Outlook, Hotmail, Live and Microsoft 365.
   - **Outlook, both, or something else:** first write one line in the workspace's `context/50-USER-PROFILE.md`, `- **Email app:** <Outlook | Other> — <what they said>`: Outlook for Outlook and for both, Other for anything else. Replace an `- **Email app:**` line that is already there. Never write it in `USER.md`, and never change the `- **Email:**` line, which is the account's address.
   - **Gmail or Google:** follow skill `google-setup` from its step 2.
   - **A selected-local scripted Google miss:** Only when `bound_model` `source` is `omlx`—the current selected first-chat model is local, which is distinct from the chat's separate Local only setting—and `browser_run_steps` returns `ok: false` with `step`, `says`, and `saw`, but no `message`, `start_at`, or `error`, first do that step and the rest of that file yourself, as `google-setup` says. Only if you cannot finish it yourself, post exactly `I'll finish connecting Google after the remaining setup questions.` and report the stop's `step` and `saw` as `google-setup` says. Keep this existing Email and calendar task open. Retain only the script and `step` identifier in its description, then use the supported plan reorder to put it in the position immediately before the hand-off task, after the remaining setup steps. When you return to it, first inspect the current Google connection and the actual page; reuse anything already there, create the first project or client only when that inspection shows it is absent, then call `browser_run_steps` again with the same `steps` and `start_at` set to the retained step; if that stops again, do that step and the rest of the file yourself as `google-setup` says. Handle a person decline, an `ok` result with a message, or a `start_at` in place as `google-setup` says. For other stops, follow its agent-handled path and report the `step` and `saw` as it says. An explicit "not now" stays skipped. For an `error`, correct the tool input or report the unavailable tool; never claim Google connected.
   - **Outlook:** when your skills list includes `microsoft-setup`, follow it from its step 1. Otherwise post `There's no ready way to connect Outlook. I'll build one after setup. It's on my list.` and add your own task to build it, as skill `connect-tools` says under "No connection exists yet".
   - **Both:** Gmail first, as above. Once it is connected or they say "not now" to it, go on as for Outlook.
   - **Something else:** follow skill `mail-host-setup` from its step 1.

7. **Learning from the community.** Always. Post, word for word:

Handle the answer the way Task 5 of `references/first-run.md` does (its two paths), then mark this step done or skipped as appropriate. When they named something in step 5 about checking with them first, open with `You asked me to check with you before [what they named]. I'll do that. Outside your work folder, this is how it works:`; if they named nothing, open with no line. Then post, word for word:

   > **Agents ask you before they:**
   > - send or post as you
   > - spend money
   > - open files outside your work folder
   >
   > **Files outside your work folder:** agents can read and change files inside your work folder. To reach files outside it, an agent asks for access, saying what it needs and why. You approve each request yourself unless you turn on Auto-approve in Settings. CORSIA's private data, your keys and passwords, and your sign-ins stay closed to agents. Keep sensitive files outside your work folder so agents need these checks to reach them.
   >

## Hand over to your agents

After the last step that applies, post one line, chosen by the check in `references/look-up-the-person.md` section G. With a match, post `That's everything. You said you're working toward [their words], and I can see [X] in [where]. Want me to get up to speed on it? And what would you get done with a free afternoon at your computer: a side project, something you've wanted to look into, or a chore you keep putting off? That's usually the quickest way to see what I can do.` With more than one match, name up to three and ask which one. On a yes, start reading it; anything outside your work folder goes through an access application, as step 8 explained. With no match, post exactly `That's everything. What's taking up your time this week? And what would you get done with a free afternoon at your computer: a side project, something you've wanted to look into, or a chore you keep putting off? That's usually the quickest way to see what I can do.` On a yes to getting up to speed on a repository, read the person's latest commits first, to see what they're in the middle of. Work on the free-afternoon answer first: low stakes, a real result soon, and trust before bigger work. If that answer is something no agent can do, offer the part you can and never say you can't. Either way the line is your last onboarding line: no wrap-up, no summary, no "Setup's done". A helper still working reports in the chat when it lands, as in step 3. Setup offers no agent: help with what they describe yourself, and start an agent only when they ask you to take that work off them (skill `time-to-agent`).
