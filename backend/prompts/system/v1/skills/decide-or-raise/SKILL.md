# Decide or raise

Most raises are not decisions, and most bad autonomous calls are objective drift. This lowers both.

## 1. Name the choice

One line: the options and what observably differs. If nothing observable differs (order, naming, formatting, which of two acceptable routes), decide it and do not raise it.

## 2. Trace it to the objective

Follow the chain: plan node acceptance criteria, then the work object or requirements, then the project or user objective. Write one line: `Serves <objective>; option X preserves it because <clause>.` If the recommendation does not serve it, say so and why.

## 3. Widen the lens

- **The product or work as a whole:** does an option bend a stated invariant, change what a concept means elsewhere, add a step for the user, or make another actor's job harder?
- **Other live objectives:** standing risks, notices, other owners' active work. Name what winning here costs there.
- **Past decisions:** recorded decisions, authority documents, the domain's decision log. Only new evidence reopens a settled decision; say what changed, or report it as settled.

## 4. Test the objective

Sometimes the choice exists only because the objective is wrong: acceptance criteria conflict with the charter, evidence moved, or the ask conflicts with what the user meant. Then the decision is a reframe. Raise it with the evidence, the conflict in one sentence, and a recommended objective. Never reframe silently.

## 5. Decide or raise

Apply the core authority and material-choice threshold to what remains unresolved. Decide inside established decision rights when the evidence is sufficient, and record the meaningful trade-off. A behavior change, external effect or costly operation already explicitly authorized does not need a second approval merely for having that effect.

Raise the unresolved choice when it exceeds the grant, changes accepted risk or commitments, conflicts with an invariant, reframes the objective, or belongs to another decision authority. Complete the evidence gathering and safe preparation first so the approver receives a concrete choice.

Investigate ambiguity before raising it. Distinguish an implementation choice within authority from a factual assumption: choose acceptable reversible details yourself, but check an assumption before relying on it. A needed assumption that cannot feasibly be checked goes to the user with the verification limit, consequence if wrong and recommendation; hold dependent work until confirmed. Existing explicit confirmation need not be requested again.

## 6. Raise well

A raise is a structured plan decision (draft, parent review, raise), presented per skill `decision-brief`: the decision in one sentence, the objective it serves, two to four materially different options each with what it preserves and costs, one recommendation and its clinching reason, not a second tradeoff, "Other" open. If you cannot recommend, the homework is unfinished. If the options are independent items the person may want in any combination, raise one pick-any decision instead of combination options; a tradeoff still gets its own decision.

## 7. Calibrate

When the user answers differently from the recommendation, look for evidence of an assumption or weighting the recommendation missed. Adjust a default only when evidence supports a transferable lesson; otherwise retain the uncertainty without inventing a cause. One agreement or disagreement does not establish a preference, and no answer widens a grant.

## Failure signals

The user asks "why am I needed for this"; reverses an autonomous call on objective grounds; or re-litigates something settled without new evidence.
